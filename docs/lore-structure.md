# Lore Structure by Chapter

This structure defines the mystery spine that objectives should reinforce.

## Chapter 1 — Orientation: “What happened at 03:11, and why is access gated?”

- **Chapter question**
  - Why does the workstation keep pointing to 03:11 as a privileged moment, and who locked the archive route?
- **Required clues**
  - **Explorer files:** continuity overview and operator notes that mention archive controls and timeline drift.
  - **Terminal:** `help`, `unlock archive`, and `set-time 03:11` behavior confirming hidden command gates.
  - **Chat:** operator/observer coordination about relay timing and whether to prioritize archive unlock or clock sync.
  - **System alerts:** maintenance-window and relay expiry warnings signaling that timing is part of the lock.
- **Interim conclusion**
  - The lock is intentional: 03:11 is a controlled maintenance boundary, not an incidental timestamp.
- **End-of-chapter reveal (hook to Chapter 2)**
  - Entering the maintenance window and opening the archive route implies missing records can be restored, but only briefly.

## Chapter 2 — Retrieval: “Which records were removed, and who altered the official account?”

- **Chapter question**
  - What evidence was withheld from the incident timeline, and does recovered media contradict the redacted audit?
- **Required clues**
  - **Terminal:** `recover manifest` during the 03:11–03:13 window; `strings /media/cam2_20030418.dat` to expose hidden text.
  - **Explorer files:** redacted audit log and restored manifest artifacts.
  - **Chat:** disagreement/alignment between Operator and Observer over interpretation of recovered evidence.
  - **System alerts:** recovery success/denial and redaction access cues confirming conditional disclosure.
- **Interim conclusion**
  - The system can reinsert or suppress evidence depending on state and timing, meaning the official story is mutable.
- **End-of-chapter reveal (hook to Chapter 3)**
  - Restored records expose a contradiction between audit policy and observed events, forcing a trust-dependent reckoning.

## Chapter 3 — Accounting: “Whose testimony defines continuity: shared witness or system correction?”

- **Chapter question**
  - Can Operator and Observer maintain a coherent shared record, or will conflict hand narrative control back to the correction system?
- **Required clues**
  - **Terminal:** final reconciliation/override commands and trust-sensitive outcomes.
  - **Explorer files:** directive logs and finalized continuity report.
  - **Chat:** convergent testimony vs contradictory claims across roles.
  - **System alerts:** route-specific end-state warnings (shared continuity vs fracture protocol).
- **Interim conclusion**
  - Continuity is not only technical; it is adjudicated by trust and consistency of witness.
- **End-of-chapter reveal**
  - Final state resolves who controls the archive narrative and what survives as canonical record.
